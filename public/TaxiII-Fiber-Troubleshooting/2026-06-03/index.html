Technical

Scope & method

A 1 Gbps fiber plan is intermittently delivering 5–75 Mbps (recovering to 500–600 Mbps) across three Wi-Fi clients, first noticed on/about June 1, 2026 — earlier sub-threshold episodes can’t be ruled out — with no clear diurnal pattern.

All diagnostics were run June 3, 2026 (conducted collaboratively with Claude AI, Anthropic) from a single always-on Mac mini (macOS) associated to the building Wi-Fi (no wired drop is available in the unit), using ping, traceroute, system_profiler, netstat, and openssl.   • Loss 20–56% end-to-end packet loss to public anchors; loss-dominated (RTT stable 3–13 ms). TCP goodput collapses under AIMD → the observed 5–75 Mbps floors.
  • Topology Loss originates at hop 1 and compounds upstream. Gateway = Axon Networks MDU CPE; service delivered over a recently “split” feed.
  • Trend First observed this week; clean baseline May 30 (0% loss, ~280 Mbps). Onset is observational — sub-threshold loss during low-demand periods could predate detection. Non-stationary (23%→56% in ~20 min); fleet-wide via shared AP.

Plain English

A lot of my data is getting lost

Next I measured how much data actually makes it through. Think of the connection as a delivery route — when I sent test packages along it, somewhere between one-in-five and more than half never arrived.

That’s the heart of the problem. When data is lost, the connection automatically slows itself down to recover — which is exactly why a gigabit line ends up feeling like 5–75 Mbps.

Plain English

The losses start right at the first box

I traced the route step by step. The losses are already happening at the very first piece of equipment my connection touches — and they get worse as the data travels onward toward the wider internet.

That points the finger at the equipment and wiring closest to me, rather than at the far-off internet.

Technical

Loss originates at hop 1 and compounds upstream

Per-hop loss in Sample B: gateway 32% → first WAN hop 40% → destination 56%. The monotonic increase indicates multiple contributors: the first local segment plus added upstream loss across the access/aggregation path.

Honest caveat: ICMP to an intermediate hop can be inflated by control-plane policing, so per-hop figures are indicative. The authoritative signal is the end-to-end loss to public anchors (20–56%). Traceroute shows no second RFC1918 hop (hop 2 is already public Lumen/CenturyLink), so no extra NAT layer is in the L3 path.

Technical

Regression vs. a known-clean baseline

Baseline on the same client, May 30, 2026: ~280 Mbps download, 0% loss, low latency, no bufferbloat under load. The degradation was first noticed this week, but onset is observational: intermittent loss during low-demand periods can pass unnoticed, so the true start may predate detection — the May 30 clean run bounds it loosely, not precisely. Loss is non-stationary (23%→56% within ~20 min), and all my devices share the same managed AP — consistent with a shared upstream impairment.

Plain English

A reboot helps — but only briefly, and only that one machine

When I restart my Mac mini, the speed jumps up for a little while, then slowly fades back down. Restarting one device doesn’t help any of the others in the home.

To me that hints the slowdown isn’t my computer itself — it’s something about how the shared building connection hands out speed to each device.

Technical

Reboot-correlated transient recovery, per-client

Power-cycling the Mac mini yields a brief throughput recovery that decays over minutes; other clients see no benefit from one device’s reboot. Per-device + transient is a fingerprint of per-client scheduling / shaping on a contended shared feed — e.g. a fresh Wi-Fi re-association resetting the AP’s airtime/rate state, or a per-session burst allowance that refills on a new DHCP lease/session and then throttles to the shaped rate.

Caveat: line variance is high, so this is suggestive, not conclusive (a Wi-Fi off/on toggle should reproduce it without a full reboot if it’s the network layer). Either way it points to the managed-MDU / shared-feed layer, not a device fault.

Specific items to verify

  • Provisioning: confirm the ONT/account shows the expected 1 Gbps service profile.
  • Optical & errors: read optical Rx power (dBm) and FEC/CRC/uncorrectable counters at the ONT/CPE for my unit. Low Rx power or rising errors confirm a degraded physical feed.
  • The split feed: verify whether a splitter, shared run, or marginal splice was introduced at connect time, and whether it’s within optical-budget spec.
  • Sharing: check for over-subscription on that PON segment that could cause intermittent loss under neighbor load.
